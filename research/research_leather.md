## 0. The core answer: The Row lambskin vs. an Etsy "lambskin" bag

Both bags can honestly say "lambskin". What separates them is everything else:

1. **Grade and finish of the skin.** It can be aniline or semi-aniline full grain from a top tannery, or a corrected, pigment-coated, embossed skin. Raw lambskin ranges from about ₹140/sq ft in India to about $18-27/sq ft retail for European plongé (section 1). The coating hides defects and changes how the skin ages (Tusting, Popov).
2. **Construction.** This covers turned (rembordé) or multi-coat painted edges versus raw or single-dip edges; stitch density and stitch type; skiving; leather versus fabric lining; reinforcement at the handles; and solid versus plated hardware (section 3).
3. **Labour hours and where the work is done.** A Hermès bag takes 15-24 hours from one artisan. A Chanel flap takes 15-20 hours and more than 180 operations. Factory-made DTC bags carry an estimated $40-100 of labour (section 4). Labour costs per hour run from €44.7 in France to €18.3 in Portugal, against roughly $1/h minimum in Vietnam.
4. **Even luxury bags have small production costs.** Teardown estimates put leather at $15-60 for a bag (about 7-12 sq ft) and total production at roughly $66-180 for DTC and premium bags. A Milan court case reported Dior subcontractor goods supplied for as little as €53 against €2,600+ retail (section 5). On a $1,700-1,850 The Row lambskin bag, materials and labour are probably a small share of the price. **No teardown of a The Row bag was found** (see Gaps).

### Observable tells a shopper can photograph or check

Each tell is listed as **what to do**, then **what it means**.

1. **Look at the stitch line close up.** A slanted stitch with the same look front and back points to a saddle stitch. A dead-straight stitch with a different-looking underside points to a lockstitch (Popov).
2. **Count stitches across 1 inch (2.5 cm) with a ruler in the photo.** On luxury quilted bags this is about 10-12. Fewer than about 8 on a fine bag is a warning sign (Romestation). Note that dense machine stitching is not proof on its own.
3. **Check for loose ends, gaps and wandering lines,** especially on the base and at the handles (Senreve teardown, Aero).
4. **Look at a cut edge.** Fuzzy fibres mean a raw edge (low tier). A glassy, smooth, even edge is burnished or multi-coat painted. If you can't see any cut edge because the grain wraps around and is stitched, it is turned (top tier). Also look for cracking or lifting edge paint at the bends (Pampeano, Purse Maison).
5. **Look at the grain under raking light.** Pores, slight shade variation and fat wrinkles mean full or aniline grain. A perfectly uniform, embossed, glossy or plastic surface means corrected or split leather (Popov, Tusting).
6. **Look inside the bag.** Leather lining, a heavy twill lining or a flimsy synthetic lining, and whether the seams are even (The Row listing, Alibaba guide).
7. **Check the hardware:**
   - Try a magnet. If it sticks, the hardware is plated steel.
   - Check weight in the hand. Heavy suggests brass; light and non-magnetic suggests zinc.
   - Look for casting seams.
   - Look for brass showing at wear points.
   - Check whether the zip is a branded RiRi, Lampo or YKK Excella (Journeyman HQ, Put This On, Polène teardown).
8. **Check handle attachment points.** Look for stacked layers, tack stitches, washers behind rivets, and whether you can feel a support plate (Articles of Style).
9. **Read the "made in" and fibre-content labels.** "Genuine leather" usually signals a split (Popov). Check whether a lining fabric is declared.
10. **For jackets:**
    - Count the panels on sleeves and back. Many small panels allow smaller skins and better yield; this is neutral for quality, but a cost clue.
    - Check the lining is split, with cupro sleeves.
    - Look for topstitch thickness, a 2-way zip, set-in shoulder construction, and whether the shoulder pads are real or faux (Effortless Gent, Stridewise).

## Gaps and caveats

1. **No B2B tannery price lists** were found for Haas, Alran, Degermann, Walpier or the Tuscan Vera Pelle consortium. Prices here come from retailers who resell to small makers. Spanish lambskin tanneries were not covered. Lambskin from top French tanneries (Alran agneau, Degermann) had no fetched price.
2. **No The Row teardown or cost estimate** exists in the fetched sources. The Row construction specs (edge type, SPI, hardware metal) were not published on the listings.
3. **Mass-factory hours per bag** (China, India, Vietnam) were not found. Only teardown dollar estimates exist for labour.
4. **Leather jacket sq ft:** only weak sources (an unsourced FAQ giving 30-50 sq ft, and a 2010 forum post about shearling flight jackets). There is no solid figure for a fashion lambskin biker jacket, and no jacket cost teardown was found.
5. **SPI benchmarks:** only Chanel-specific reseller data and a hobby-forum post. There is no cross-brand published SPI standard.
6. **Hardware tells** come from a home-hardware article. The magnet and weight logic transfers to bags, but its price multiples (3-5x) are for household fittings.
7. **The Spain and Portugal labour tie to leather goods** is general labour-cost data only. The Spanish (Ubrique) and Portuguese leather-goods collective agreements were not fetched. No hourly figure for Indian leather workers was fetched (only monthly).
8. **FX conversions** (INR, CNY, KRW, GBP to USD/CAD) were not fetched. Apply live rates.
9. **Senreve, Mansur Gavriel and Cuyana teardown pages are undated.** Several Tanner Leatherstein figures are one person's estimates, not brand disclosures. Everlane's published components don't sum to its stated totals.
10. **No investigative piece** gives a lambskin-specific luxury vs. copy cost comparison. The Dior €53 figure is per item supplied, and the article doesn't specify which bag.
