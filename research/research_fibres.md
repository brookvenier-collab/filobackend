# Filo: raw fibre and fabric cost inputs (fair retail price model)

Research date: 2026-10-19. Every figure below came from a page fetched during this session; the URL is recorded beside it. Prices are given in the currency and unit the source used. Conversions are marked "(conv.)": 1 lb = 0.4536 kg, so ¢/lb x 0.02205 = USD/kg.

**How to read confidence**
- **High:** an official or primary market report (USDA, AWEX/AWI, Egyptian Cotton Association, CELC/Alliance, PROMPERÚ/SUNAT).
- **Medium:** a reputable secondary aggregator, a dated trade article, or a live supplier listing that states its specs.
- **Low:** a blog or marketing page, an aggregator that labels itself "sample/illustrative", or figures that conflict with better sources.

**Cross-fibre benchmark caveat.** Textile Tech Insight's "Global Fibre Prices 2026" (Sept 2026) is the only single page that covers every fibre. Several of its figures disagree with primary data:
- It puts cashmere at about $58/kg. Live dehaired listings sit at $100-143/kg.
- It puts silk at about $39/kg. Silk indices sit at about $58-68/kg.

Use it only as a fallback (low confidence). Source: https://textiletechinsight.com/article/global-fibre-prices-2026/

**Why the better grade is better.** Longer and finer fibres can be spun into finer, stronger and smoother yarn, with fewer fibre ends to work loose and pill.
- **Pima/Supima:** Supima averages over 1 7/16". The extra-long-staple threshold is 1 3/8". Pima is about 3% of the US crop and about 1% of world cotton ([Textile World, Apr 2017](https://www.textileworld.com/textile-world/knitting-apparel/2017/04/5-surprising-facts-about-supima-cotton/)).
- **Sea Island:** staple of about 36-37 mm against roughly 20-25 mm for commodity cotton, with micronaire about 3.1-3.4 (finer). Only about 150 bales are produced a year ([Orlebar Brown](https://www.orlebarbrown.com/blog/why-sea-island-cotton-is-the-rarest-in-the-world-blog.html); [Graccioza, Sep 2026](https://www.graccioza.com/us/why-is-sea-island-cotton-so-expensive_815.html?idb=41)).
- **Grade matters within Pima too:** low-grade Pima (colour/leaf 6) costs about the same as upland. A "Pima" label alone does not guarantee a premium fibre.

**Why finer is better.** Finer fibres bend away from the skin instead of poking it.
- Coarse fibres above about 30-32 µ cause prickle. Superfine is about ≤17.5 µ and ultrafine about ≤16 µ ([Woolmark](https://www.woolmark.com/fibre/what-is-the-wool-fibre/is-wool-comfortable-for-sensitive-skin/)).
- The "comfort factor" is the share of fibres under 30 µ. Even about 5% coarse fibres reads as scratchy, so the spread of diameters matters as well as the mean ([Black Bear Brand, Aug 2026](https://blackbearbrand.com/blog-1/2026/8/22/wool-measured-in-microns)).
- Super-S numbers are legally tied to maximum fibre diameter under the US Wool Products Labeling Act, aligned with IWTO: Super 100s ≤18.75 µ, 120s ≤17.75, 130s ≤17.25, 150s ≤16.25 ([Wikipedia: S number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S_number_(wool))).
- The price steps are steep. 17 µ costs about 2.5x 26 µ and about 3.4x 28 µ per clean kg.

Legal and industry definitions:
- **Legal ceiling (US):** mean diameter ≤19 µ, CV ≤24%, and ≤3% by weight over 30 µ ([CCMI](https://cashmere.org/definition-cashmere-wool.php); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashmere_wool)).
- **Italian-spun reference:** Luca Faloni states its Cariaggi-spun yarn uses 15.5 µ fibre averaging up to 40 mm, from Inner Mongolia, spun in Cagli ([lucafaloni.com](https://lucafaloni.com/pages/craft/cashmere)).

**Why better is better.** Two things set the grade:
- **Fineness (lower µ):** softer hand.
- **Fibre length:** longer fibres are held more firmly in the yarn. Short fibres work loose and form pills, which is the main reason cheap cashmere pills ([Gobi Cashmere](https://us.gobicashmere.com/blogs/news/cashmere-pilling-causes-prevention-fixes)).

Live listings show a premium of about $35-40/kg for 13.8 µ over about 15 µ fibre. A 36 mm white fibre also costs more than a 34 mm brown one.

**Why better is better.** Mulberry (cultivated) cocoons reel into one continuous filament of up to about 1,000 m. Wild or tussah silk is shorter, less even and naturally beige, and sells for much less ([Promeed](https://promeed.com/blogs/secret-behind-silk/difference-between-mulberry-silk-and-tussah-silk-sheets), seller content).

"6A" is trade shorthand rather than an ISO/ASTM standard. It signals long unbroken filament (about 600-900 m), even colour and very few slubs ([Murier Silk](https://muriersilk.com/blog/6a-grade-silk-explained)).

Momme measures fabric weight, not fibre grade:
- 19 mm is light.
- 22 mm is the standard for pillowcases.
- 25 mm and up is dense and more durable.

Heavier momme means proportionally more raw silk per metre.

**Why better is better.**
- **Long fibre vs tow:** long line fibre costs roughly 3-4x tow and spins into smooth, strong, fine yarn. Tow makes coarser yarn.

Both labels are about origin and traceability, not a measured quality grade.

## Gaps (not sourced, or only weakly)

   - No independent index (Schneider's public reports are 2018-2019 and have no prices).
   - Dehaired prices come from supplier listings only.

   - Chinese spot quotes (SunSirs, Jiaxing index) were blocked by a security check.
